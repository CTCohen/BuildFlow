## Detailed Competitive Landscape

### DIY Builders (Consumer-grade)
- **Wix:** $12-27/mo
- **Squarespace:** $16-33/mo
- **GoDaddy:** $5-20/mo
- **Signal:** "Cheap, experimental, DIY, template-based"

### Managed SaaS (Professional, some support)
- **Jobber:** $49-349/mo (CRM-first, website is secondary)
- **ServiceTitan:** $298+/mo (CRM-first, website is secondary)
- **Housecall Pro:** $79-259/mo (CRM + lightweight site)
- **10Web:** $49-299/mo (AI assistant, not full automation)
- **Signal:** "Professional business software"

### BuildFlow (AI-generated + done-for-you + CRM)
- **Micro:** $149/mo (below ServiceTitan, 2.5x DIY)
- **SMB:** $249/mo (within boutique agency range, 50-75% less than full agency)

- **Signal:** "Premium automation — done-for-you with AI, CRM-integrated, design quality"

### Agencies (Custom/semi-custom)
- **Freelancer:** $500-2,500 one-time (4-8 weeks)
- **Small Agency:** $1,500-5,000 one-time + $100-300/mo retainer
- **Boutique Agency:** $3,000-10,000 one-time + $300-800/mo retainer
- **Signal:** "Bespoke, white-glove, long timeline"

1. **It's above the "experimental" threshold** ($99/mo sounds like you're trying it; $249/mo sounds like you're buying professional software)
2. **It's the Slack/HubSpot pricing tier** (buyers know this price point = real support)
3. **It's $2,988/year vs. $2-5k agency one-time** (cheaper than agency, more serious than DIY)
4. **It justifies support & operations** (margin to pay support team, invest in product)
5. **It attracts quality buyers** (people who care about quality, not just price)

## Strategic Recommendations

### 1. Pricing Is Right; Messaging Is Critical

**Your current pricing ($149-399/mo) is solid.** But success depends on messaging:

**DON'T say:**
- "Only $249/mo" (sounds cheap, undermines quality signal)
- "Cheaper than agencies" (frames it as a discount, not a product category)
- "AI-built website" (sounds experimental, unfinished)

**DO say:**
- "Done-for-you website that talks to your CRM. Built in a minute, live in an hour."
- "Professional automation for established service businesses."
- "Not DIY. Not full agency. The fast path between them."

### 2. Tier Assignment Matters More Than Price

**Make tier assignment deterministic via scoring, not user choice:**
- Micro = solopreneurs + under $50k revenue
- SMB = 3-20 employees, $250k-$5M revenue (YOUR ICP)
- Mid-Market = 20+ employees, $5M+ revenue (paused for now)

**Why:** Buyers at wrong tier see wrong value prop and churn.

### 3. Price Increase Strategy Is Smart

**Current plan: Launch at current prices, raise in 2-3 months**

**Why this works:**
1. Validates conversion rate at current price
2. Doesn't leave money on table (if $249 SMB converts well, raise to $299-349)
3. Grandfathering early customers builds loyalty
4. Rising prices signal "we're popular; demand is high"

**Recommendation: Set clear trigger**
- Launch: $149/$249/$399
- After 25 customers OR Month 3 (whichever comes first): Raise to $199/$349/$599
- Keep first 25-50 customers grandfathered

### 4. Micro Tier Is Interesting but Risky

**Current: Micro $149/mo**
- Competitive with ServiceTitan ($298+), but solo contractors can't afford $1.8k/year
- DIY builders ($12-50/mo) are the real Micro competition
- **Recommendation:** Keep Micro at $149/mo for brand integrity; accept lower Micro attach rate

**Alternative: Micro $99/mo (if margin allows)**
- Softens the "budget" signal but competes with Jobber
- Would require strong SMB positioning to avoid tier cannibalization

### 5. Offboard Pricing Is Solid

**Current: $499 Micro, $799 SMB**
- $799 = 3.2 months of Managed ($249 × 3.2)
- Competes well vs. agency one-time ($1.5-5k)
- Signals "affordable ownership"

**No change recommended** (prices are rational).
