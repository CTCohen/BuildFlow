# Prospect Research Template & Guide

**Purpose:** Systematize prospect research so you can fill this in and immediately launch outreach.

Use this template to research and track 10 target contractors for Phase 1 outreach.

---

## Prospect Research Spreadsheet

**Create a Google Sheet or Airtable with these columns:**

```
| # | First Name | Business Name | Email | Phone | Trade | City | Website | Google Rank | Current Site Quality | Notes | Email Sent | Opens | Clicks | Call Booked | Closed |
|---|-----------|--------------|-------|-------|-------|------|---------|-------------|----------------------|-------|-----------|-------|--------|------------|--------|
| 1 | Mike | Phoenix HVAC Pro | mike@... | 602-... | HVAC | Phoenix | hvac-pro.com | Position 8 | Basic (2015 design) | No mobile | ☐ | | | ☐ | ☐ |
| 2 | Sarah | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ☐ | | | ☐ | ☐ |
```

**Columns explained:**
- `#` — Prospect number (1-10)
- `First Name` — Use in email greeting
- `Business Name` — Exact legal name
- `Email` — Contact email (required for outreach)
- `Phone` — Contact phone (optional, for SMS follow-up)
- `Trade` — HVAC, Plumbing, Electrical, Cleaning, etc.
- `City` — Service area (use in email: "contractors in [City]")
- `Website` — Their current website URL (if exists)
- `Google Rank` — Position on Google Maps for "[Trade] [City]" (if ranked)
- `Current Site Quality` — Assessment (no site, outdated, basic, professional)
- `Notes` — Competitor names, any personal research
- `Email Sent` — Checkbox (when you send)
- `Opens` — Track if they opened email
- `Clicks` — Track if they clicked portfolio link
- `Call Booked` — Checkbox (if they booked a call)
- `Closed` — Checkbox (if they became a customer)

## How to Research Each Prospect (6 min per person)

### Step 1: Find the Business (2 min)
**Google Maps search:**
```
[Trade] [City]
Example: "HVAC Phoenix" or "plumber Denver"
```

**Result:** Scroll through top 10 results. Find small contractors (1-5 person teams).

**What to copy:**
- Business name (exact)
- Phone number
- Address/service area
- Website URL (if they have one)

### Step 2: Find the Email (2 min)

**Try these in order:**

1. **Website → Contact page**
   - If they have a site, look for "Contact Us" page
   - Email usually listed there
   - Example: "contact@businessname.com" or "hello@businessname.com"

2. **LinkedIn → Business page**
   - Search business name on LinkedIn
   - Click "Employees" or "Follow"
   - Find owner/manager profile
   - Look for "info" or email on their profile

3. **LinkedIn → Owner personal profile**
   - Search "[First Name] [Business Name]"
   - Click on owner profile
   - Sometimes email is in "About" section

4. **Guess the email pattern**
   - If website is "businessname.com", try:
   - `contact@businessname.com`
   - `hello@businessname.com`
   - `info@businessname.com`
   - `[firstname]@businessname.com`

5. **Facebook Business page**
   - Search business name on Facebook
   - Look for "Contact" or "Message" button
   - May have email listed

6. **If all else fails**
   - Use SMS (phone number from Google Maps)
   - Or skip and move to next prospect

### Step 3: Assess Current Website (1 min)

Visit their website (if they have one):
- Does it look modern? (Responsive, clean design)
- Is it mobile-friendly? (View on phone)
- Are there clear CTAs? (Quote buttons, phone numbers)
- Do they appear on Google Maps? (Check "Google Rank")

**Rate as:**
- "None" = No website
- "Outdated" = Website from 2015-2018, looks old
- "Basic" = Functional but generic, template-like
- "Professional" = Modern, well-designed (rare for small contractors)

**Use in email:** If they have an outdated site, mention it: "I noticed your site is from 2015..."

### Step 4: Find a Competitor (1 min)

Search "[Trade] [City]" on Google Maps again.

**Find a competitor they might lose business to:**
- Someone with a nicer website
- Someone ranking higher on Google
- Someone who looks more professional

**Use in email subject line:** "[Name], [Competitor Name] is ranking higher than you on Google"

**How to find competitor name:**
- Google Maps shows ranked results (1st, 2nd, 3rd place)
- Look at top 3 results
- Pick the one with best website/ranking
- Use their exact business name in email

## Email Personalization Checklist

- [ ] Prospect first name (in greeting)
- [ ] Competitor name (in subject line + body)
- [ ] Their service type (HVAC/plumbing/etc)
- [ ] Their city (in email body: "contractors in [City]")
- [ ] Their email address (verified working)

## Research Speed Hacks

**To research 10 people in 1 hour (6 min each):**

1. **Open 3 tabs:**
   - Tab 1: Google Maps
   - Tab 2: LinkedIn
   - Tab 3: Your spreadsheet

2. **For each prospect:**
   - Google Maps: Find name, phone, business name, website
   - Their website: Copy email + assess quality
   - LinkedIn: Verify owner name
   - Spreadsheet: Fill in row
   - Notes: Competitor name

3. **Batch the research:**
   - Do all 10 at once (don't switch contexts)
   - Use same search pattern for all
   - Speed up over time (first 2-3 slow, rest fast)

4. **If stuck on email:**
   - Skip that prospect, come back later
   - Or use LinkedIn message instead
   - Or try guessing email pattern

## Prospect Selection Strategy

**Choose across multiple trades (don't do 10 HVAC contractors):**

**Tier 1 (Your strength):**
- 2-3 contractors in your favorite trade
- Research these first (you know the pain points)

**Tier 2 (Easy wins):**
- 2-3 contractors in a nearby city
- Easier to find + already have frameworks

**Tier 3 (Diversity):**
- 2-3 different trades (electrical, plumbing, cleaning)
- Learn patterns across industries

**Tier 4 (Stretch):**
- 1-2 in less common trades
- Higher price positioning possible

**Example mix:**
- 3 HVAC (Phoenix)
- 2 Plumbing (Denver)
- 2 Electrical (Austin)
- 2 Cleaning (LA area)
- 1 Pressure washing (random city)

**Why diverse?** You'll see which trades respond best + refine messaging per industry.
