# Service Detail Pages — What Real Customers Need

## Rule
Service detail page = "Is this for me? What happens? How much?" Structure: (1) Problem statement (2) Process/symptoms (3) Pricing signal (4) Real objections answered.

## Why
Customers landing on "AC Repair" page don't know if it's their problem. Specific problem list ("compressor dead? refrigerant leak? capacitor failed?") signals expertise and saves time. Pricing transparency kills "am I getting overcharged" anxiety.

## Page Structure

### Section 1: Problem Statement
**Problem headline:** State the specific failure mode.
- HVAC: "AC isn't cooling, making noise, or blowing warm air?"
- Plumbing: "Burst pipe, clogged drain, or water bill spike?"

**Subheading:** Reassure. "We've fixed this 500+ times."

### Section 2: What's Actually Happening (Symptoms List)
**Title:** "What we diagnose" or "What this might be"

**Format:** Bullet list of real problems this service fixes.

HVAC example:
- Compressor failed (won't cycle on/off)
- Refrigerant leak (low charge, weak cooling)
- Capacitor blown (unit won't start)
- Clogged filter or coil (reduced airflow)
- Thermostat malfunction (won't turn on/off)
- Fan motor dead (no airflow, noisy clicking)

Plumbing example:
- Tree roots in sewer line (slow drains, backups)
- Pipe corrosion or scale (low pressure, discolored water)
- Clogged drain (water won't drain, smell)
- Burst or split line (water pooling, high bill)
- Thermostat malfunction (won't heat or cool)
- P-trap dry or cracked (smell, pest entry)

### Section 3: Our Process
**Title:** "How we fix it" or "What to expect"

**Format:** Numbered steps. Be specific.

HVAC example:
1. Diagnosis ($99 service call, waived if you book repair)
2. Visual inspection of outdoor and indoor units
3. Electrical testing (voltage, amperage, continuity)
4. Refrigerant pressure check
5. Explain findings in person; show the problem if visible
6. Upfront quote before any work
7. Repair performed same day (usually 1–3 hours)
8. Testing and warranty paperwork

Plumbing example:
1. Emergency call answered in minutes
2. We come prepared (most tools needed in truck)
3. Problem diagnosis (often with video camera for $50 extra)
4. Explain what we found; show photos if possible
5. Upfront estimate before any digging
6. Work performed same day or scheduled
7. Clean up and testing

### Section 4: Pricing Transparency
**Title:** "Pricing" or "Cost range"

**Format:** Honest range, not fake "$79 special."

HVAC example:
- Service call: $89 (waived if you book repair)
- Typical repair: $300–$800 (parts + labor)
- Compressor replacement: $1,200–$2,000
- 1-year parts & labor warranty

Plumbing example:
- Emergency call: $125 (applies to bill if you book service)
- Drain clearing: $200–$400
- Leak repair (no excavation): $300–$600
- Burst pipe (includes excavation): $1,500–$3,500
- 1-year parts & labor warranty

### Section 5: Real Objections (FAQ)
**Title:** "Common questions"

Minimal 3 Q&A. Address the top fear.

HVAC:
- Q: "How quickly can you come?" A: "Most calls before 2pm get same-day service within 4 hours."
- Q: "Do you really fix it or just sell me a new system?" A: "We repair if it's fixable. If replacement is cheaper over 5 years, we'll say so."
- Q: "Is the warranty real?" A: "Yes. 1-year parts & labor. If something fails due to our work, we fix it free."

Plumbing:
- Q: "Will you tear up my yard?" A: "We camera first. Most leaks fixed without digging."
- Q: "What if it's worse than you said?" A: "We call with a new estimate before digging deeper."
- Q: "Do I need to be home?" A: "Yes, for emergency. For scheduled work, we can work with you."

## "Alive but Breathing" Tone

### DO:
- Use real problem names (compressor, capacitor, p-trap)
- Show you've done this 100+ times
- Admit complexity when it exists ("this might be $300, might be $2,000 depending on what we find")
- Include your specific guarantee (you decide the warranty length)
- Let personality show ("We get it — AC died on a 115-degree day")

### DON'T:
- Fake simplicity ("This is easy!")
- Generic corporate voice ("Industry-leading solutions")
- Hidden pricing ("Call for a quote!")
- Unnecessary disclaimers
- Animation overload

## Check
- [ ] Problem statement is specific, not generic
- [ ] Symptoms list shows expertise (10+ specific failure modes listed)
- [ ] Process is step-by-step and honest
- [ ] Pricing range given (not exact; that's the quote)
- [ ] At least 3 FAQ items addressing real fears
- [ ] Warranty/guarantee clearly stated
- [ ] Copy shows this has been done many times before
- [ ] No corporate jargon; honest local voice
- [ ] Mobile-readable (short paragraphs, white space)
